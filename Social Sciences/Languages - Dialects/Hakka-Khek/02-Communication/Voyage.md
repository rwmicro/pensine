# Travelling: Hotel, Transport, Money and Health

Useful sentences for getting by in Pontianak: booking a room, finding your way, paying, ordering and seeing a doctor. They are built on a handful of patterns (see the last section), so once you know *ngai hiau … mo?* and *nyi jiu … mo?* you can make your own. The romanisation follows Indonesian spelling (*c* sounds like "ch", *j* like "j" in *jam*) and marks no tones: listen to a speaker whenever you can, as one syllable can carry several meanings.

*Kien* is the room, but also the classifier of rooms: *jit kien khung* is "one free room".

## At the Hotel

| Hakka | English |
|---|---|
| Nyi jiu khung kien mo kin pu am? | Do you have a free room tonight? |
| He, jiu jit kien khung. Jiu to sa nyin? | Yes, there's one free room. How many people? |
| Ngai oi kau tai kien tu. | I'd like to book a room. |
| Nyi jiu ko phien nyin kien mo? | Do you have a cheaper room? |
| To lui loi hiet jit ja? | How much is it for one night? |
| Ka jiu sui kien ka si kong mo? | Does it have a bathroom and a toilet? |
| Kit to tiam co chon? | What time is breakfast? |
| Nga kien mang ho. | My room isn't ready yet. |
| Nga kien tu mang met chiang. | My room hasn't been cleaned. |
| Ngai en hiau to sau sui. | I can't get any hot water. |
| Mun em hiau khoi jung lia so si. | The door won't open with this key. |
| Ngai oi chut. Lia nga kien tu so. | I'm going out. Here is my room key. |

## Getting Around

| Hakka | English |
|---|---|
| Ngai hiau mai phiau khiun liak bie mo? | Can I buy a ticket here? |
| Ngai mang jiu phiau. | I don't have a ticket yet. |
| Nyi jiu con phiau mo? | Do you have a return ticket? |
| Jit to tiam kakha bis loi? | What time does the next bus come? |
| Focha thin khiun Brighton mo? | Does the train stop at Brighton? |
| Piong ngai thin tet ha cha cham. | Let me off at the next stop. |
| Lia lu ma ai miang? | What is this street called? |
| Hi chit chit kak ben co sak. | Go straight on, then turn left. |
| Chit-chit hang teu nyi khon to jok tiam. | Go straight until you see the pharmacy. |
| He, khiun cin sak, khiun ji jan kap piak. | Yes, on the right, next to the hospital. |
| Ngai oi to vikichong liong tiam. | I'd like to get to the airport at two o'clock. |
| Nyi hiau ten ngai ali mo? | Can you wait for me here? |
| Chit ka oi ten kiw mo? | Do we have to wait long? |
| Cha fai het. | The car has broken down. |
| Ngai piong het ngai su pau khiun taksi. | I left my bag in the taxi. |

## Money and Prices

| Hakka | English |
|---|---|
| Jit to lui jit sung siu mat? | How much is this pair of gloves? |
| Kie kachien sip eng dolar. | It costs fifteen dollars. |
| Ngai hiau khon ko phien nyin tung si mo? | Can I see something cheaper? |
| Ka ka han hiau nai kam mo? | Can the price still come down? |
| Nyi mai gi thet kui ka ngai. | You're selling it to me too dear. |
| Ngai oi kau lui. | I'd like to change some money. |
| Abui ngai hiau kau nga lui? | Where can I change my money? |

In *nyi mai gi thet kui*, *mai* means "to sell", whereas in *ngai oi mai* it means "to buy". In writing they are two words, 賣 and 買, which only the tone tells apart: one more reason not to trust romanisation without tones.

## Eating and Drinking

| Hakka | English |
|---|---|
| Nyi oi sit ma ai sui? | What would you like to drink? |
| Ma ai tung si nyi oi sit? | What would you like to eat? |
| Ngai boi sit cha kak nyiu nen. | I'll have tea with milk. |
| Ngai hiau to sit ciu kin to mo? | Can I have lunch now? |
| Ngai jiu si kien loi sit fon mo? | Do I have time to eat? |
| Nyong pan he ka ngai loi sit cha? | How about joining me for a drink? |

*Sit* (食) covers both eating and drinking: *sit fon*, eat rice; *sit cha*, drink tea; *sit sui*, drink water.

## Health

| Hakka | English |
|---|---|
| Ngai pot phiang. | I'm ill. |
| Ngai chi to thung ali. | It hurts here. |
| Nyi chi to theu na thung mo? | Do you have a headache? |
| Nyi hiau kai sau tu si thung jok mo? | Can you recommend something for a stomach ache? |
| Sit liak jok he nyi chi to fat sau. | Take this medicine if you feel feverish. |
| Nyi jiu sit phet thin jok mo? | Are you taking any other medicine? |
| Ngai kiang ngai khu to sit thuk. | I'm afraid I've got food poisoning. |
| Cui khiun kai ji jan abui? | Where is the nearest hospital? |

## Structures to Reuse

- **Ngai hiau … mo?** "Can I …?": *hiau* (曉, to know how, to be able) is the everyday way to say "can".
- **Nyi jiu … mo?** "Do you have …?": *jiu* (有) + noun + *mo* at the end of a question.
- **Jit to lui …?** "How much is …?": literally "how much money".
- **Ko + adjective** "more …": *ko phien nyin*, cheaper; *ko thai*, bigger.
- **Nga + noun** "my …": *nga kien*, my room; *nga lui*, my money.
